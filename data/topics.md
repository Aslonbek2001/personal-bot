## Block 1: Tenses for daily work
1. Present Simple vs Present Continuous - describing your routine and current tasks in a stand-up
2. Past Simple - reporting what you did yesterday
3. Articles a, an, the - talking about servers, bugs and features
4. Present Perfect vs Past Simple - writing status updates ("I have fixed the bug")
5. Present Perfect Continuous - explaining long tasks ("I have been debugging this for two hours")
6. Past Continuous and Past Simple - describing how a production bug happened
7. Future forms: will, going to, Present Continuous - planning sprint tasks
8. Countable and uncountable nouns (data, information, feedback, much, many) - talking about datasets

## Block 2: Questions, requests and modals
9. Question word order - asking clear questions in team chat
10. Polite requests: could you, would you mind, can I - asking teammates for help
11. Modals of ability and possibility: can, could, may, might - discussing possible solutions
12. Modals of obligation and advice: must, have to, should - writing code review comments
13. Modals of deduction: must be, might be, can't be - guessing the cause of a bug

## Block 3: Explaining systems
14. Zero and First Conditional - describing system behavior ("If the cache is empty, the service calls the database")
15. Second Conditional - discussing alternative architectures
16. Passive voice (present and past) - writing technical documentation
17. Passive voice with modals and Present Perfect - deployment notes ("The index has been rebuilt")
18. Relative clauses: who, which, that, where - describing components of a RAG pipeline
19. Linking words: because, although, however, therefore - explaining technical decisions
20. Purpose and result: to, in order to, so that, so - explaining why code exists
21. Comparatives and superlatives - comparing models, libraries and approaches

## Block 4: Natural and professional English
22. Gerunds and infinitives - making suggestions ("I suggest using", "We decided to migrate")
23. Dependent prepositions (depend on, responsible for, interested in) - describing your role and tasks
24. Phrasal verbs for developers: set up, roll back, look into, figure out
25. Indirect questions - asking politely ("Could you tell me why the build failed?")
26. Reported speech - summarizing meetings and client requests
27. Third Conditional - writing post-mortems ("If we had added monitoring, we would have noticed it")
28. Future Continuous and Future Perfect - deadlines and delivery estimates

## Block 5: Writing at work
29. Formal vs informal register - the difference between email and Slack messages
30. Writing a clear technical email - structure, requests and follow-ups
31. Writing pull request descriptions and commit messages
32. Writing documentation: README and API docs - imperative mood and clear instructions
